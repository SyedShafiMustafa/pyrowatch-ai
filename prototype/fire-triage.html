Fire detection triage

Imports satellite active-fire detections (NASA FIRMS CSV format), validates them, and ranks them with an explainable score so an analyst knows what to look at first.

How the score works, and where it fails

Score = weighted average (0–100) of five factors, each scaled 0–1: recency (exponential decay from the newest detection), confidence (low 0.2, nominal 0.6, high 1.0, or numeric/100), FRP (log-scaled against the dataset's 95th percentile), repeat detections (other detections within ~2 km in 72 h, capped at 4), and brightness (300–400 K).

  • Persistent heat sources such as industry, flares or volcanoes score high on repeats. The sample includes two such sites so you can see this.
  • Cloud, smoke and viewing angle hide fires; absence of detections is not absence of fire.
  • Pixels are 375 m to 1 km, so coordinates are approximate. Confidence labels differ between instruments.
  • Weights are judgement calls, not fitted. Evaluation on a tiny synthetic sample shows the pipeline works; it makes no claim of real-world accuracy.
